Reflective Report

What worked, what did not, and what I would do differently next time.

Reflection

The main thing I learned in this assignment was the difference between destructive and non destructive editing, and I only learned it because I did it wrong first. When I started cutting the figure out of each layer in Photoshop I used the Eraser, which left rough edges, still had grass and part of the building stuck to his shoulder, and had cut straight through his hair. The bigger issue was that the Eraser deletes the pixels themselves, so once they were gone the only way to get them back was stepping through the History panel, and I had ten layers that all needed the same job done to them.

Switching to Object Select and turning that selection into a layer mask fixed both of these problems. The selection followed the actual edge of the figure rather than my brush, and as a mask hides pixels instead of deleting them I was able to paint parts back in when it clipped a shoe. This is the same idea as the masking I had already done in Lightroom, just in a different program, and once I noticed that the two connected I started thinking about the whole pipeline differently.

My composite also did not work the first time. I built it from five frames in a row, but he only moves about half a stride between each shot, so all five figures landed on top of each other and the result looked like a mistake rather than a study of movement. Using every fifth frame instead gave the figures enough space and it worked straight away, so the method itself was never the issue, the gap between the frames was.

The thing I would do differently is my timing. I got the database, the processing and the compositing to a standard I am happy with, and then left the GIFs and the After Effects work until the last night, which is the part of the course I am most interested in and the part I gave myself the least room to experiment with.
